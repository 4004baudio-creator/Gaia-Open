The biosphere and the orbital shell are two rooms of the same house. Repair one while filling the other with unmanaged mass is incomplete.

## Present (ANCHORED class — cite ESA DISCOS / agency reports when stamping numbers)

ESA Space Debris User Portal (update window 2026): tens of thousands of catalogued objects regularly tracked; on the order of ~10⁴ tonnes of mass in Earth orbit; statistical populations of ~10⁵–10⁶ objects in the 1–10 cm class and ~10⁸ in the millimetre class. Fragmentation events still occur every year. Agency consensus (IADC / ESA Space Environment Report 2025): even with no new launches, collisions among objects already there can grow the population. That cascade is **Kessler** — a modelled attractor, not a date stamp.

Do not freeze a single public blog’s object count into chrome. Pull the current DISCOS / NASA ODPO table when the weekly check runs.

## Two requirements (meet both)

| Shell | Requirement | Failure if ignored |
|---|---|---|
| Internal biosphere | EEI → 0; sinks and methane work (Repair 1–4) | Heat content rises; repair crews lose capacity |
| External orbital shell | Usable LEO/MEO/GEO lanes; controlled end-of-life; removal of the worst hulks | Constellations and science craft take hits; exploration closes from the inside; reentry casualty risk on the ground |

Neither shell outranks the other. A methane pivot that funds more unmanaged mega-constellation mass without disposal is a half-map. An ADR mission that ignores Earth EEI is also a half-map.

## Risks if uncorrected

**Exploration / operators**
- Hypervelocity hits on working satellites and stations (cm-class can end a mission; mm-class pits optics and arrays).
- Conjunction load: more avoidance burns → less fuel for science.
- Band loss: if a shell becomes collision-dominated, that altitude is expensive or unusable for decades.
- Dual-use fear: grabber craft look like weapons. Permission of the launching state is required. GO does not treat ADR as a targeting office.

**Local Earth**
- Uncontrolled reentry of large stages and intact satellites: casualty-risk guideline commonly cited at 1 in 10,000 per event — a design limit, not a comfort.
- Surviving fragments and toxic propellant on land or sea.
- Lost climate and weather sensors if those platforms are struck.
- Kessler is not “sky fall.” It is *loss of the commons above the atmosphere*.

## GO protocol

1. **Two ledgers.** Ground repair stays Repair 1–4. Orbital repair is this file. Do not launder one as the other.
2. **Catalogue first.** A debris claim needs a catalogue ID, epoch, and source (DISCOS, SATCAT). No ID → BESIDE.
3. **Rank.** Tracked object + agency report > statistical MASTER-class estimate > market-size blog > story.
4. **Prefer prevention, then servicing, then grab.** New mass must carry a disposal contract. Servicing extends life of *working* craft. Grab is for objects that will not decay in time.
5. **Reentry is a ground event.** Controlled demise preferred. Uncontrolled large objects stay on the risk shelf.
6. **No weapon stamp.** ADR tech is dual-use. GO records the service packet (object, owner consent, method, outcome). It does not host targeting lists.
7. **Constellations are pipes.** Starlink-class coverage is ANCHORED infrastructure. Unmanaged failure modes of that shell are also ANCHORED when the operator or agency publishes them.
8. **Meet the other shell.** A builder handshake that adds launch mass must name its disposal path. A ground-repair firm that depends on EO satellites has a stake in this ledger.
